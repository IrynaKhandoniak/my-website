Hard skills vs soft skills. Why emotional intelligence is the glue.

Hard skills describe what you can do. Soft skills shape how you work with others. Emotional intelligence determines how well those abilities come together.

Emotional intelligence is the ability to manage your own emotions while understanding the emotions of people around you. It is not simply another soft skill — it is the foundation that makes other soft skills work.

Core idea: technical ability may get you into the room, but emotional intelligence shapes how effectively you contribute once you are there.

What is emotional intelligence?

Five elements that shape behaviour

04 Empathy Understand how other people may feel or experience a situation.
05 Social skills Communicate, collaborate and manage relationships effectively.

Where does EQ come from?

Emotional development over time

Age 0–3

Emotions come first

We feel before we can think. Regulation is mostly external and caregivers model calm.

  • Reactions are largely impulse-driven.
  • Safety and co-regulation shape early responses.
Around 3–7

Language appears, but emotions still lead

Children begin naming feelings and connecting triggers, emotions and actions.

  • Trigger → feeling → action becomes easier to understand.
  • Simple regulation starts: pause, breathe, ask for help.
Teens → adulthood

Maturity becomes balance

Emotion and intellect begin working together more deliberately.

  • Notice the first reaction and choose a better second response.
  • Empathy and perspective-taking support better decisions.
Practice at any stage: pause → name the feeling → choose a response. Try: “When X happens, I feel Y, so I’ll do Z.”
Emotions in practice

Your emotional palette

Imagine a small inner “monster” with five basic states. All are natural. The skill is not to suppress them, but to avoid projecting every emotion onto other people.

JoyPositive energy
SadnessLoss and reflection
AngerBoundary or frustration
FearThreat and uncertainty
CalmRegulation and clarity
Ethics in action: feel fully, respond thoughtfully.
Signs of low emotional intelligence

What poor regulation can look like

A strong need to always be right.
Ignoring or dismissing other people’s feelings.
Blaming others for personal problems.
Frequent emotional outbursts.
Constantly steering conversations back to oneself.
Impatience and irritability.
Believing that everyone around you is incompetent.
Takeaway: hard skills create capability. Soft skills create connection. Emotional intelligence helps both work together.
